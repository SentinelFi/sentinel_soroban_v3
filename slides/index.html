The problem

Flight disruption is routine.
Compensation is not.

0
% OF FLIGHTS ARRIVE LATE

Roughly one in five flights is delayed — a constant, measurable, insurable risk.

Claims are manual

Traditional travel insurance means forms, receipts, adjusters and weeks of waiting for an outcome the airline's own data already proves.

Most never claim

Opaque terms and claim friction mean a large share of entitled passengers simply give up — value that never reaches the people who paid for it.

The solution

Insurance that settles itself.

Sentinel is parametric: the policy pays a fixed amount when a measurable condition is met — the flight lands late beyond a set threshold, or is cancelled. No forms, no adjusters, no discretion.

  • Fixed terms up front. Premium, payout and delay threshold are on-chain before purchase.
  • Oracle-attested outcomes. Real flight arrival data decides — not a claims department.
  • 100% collateralized. Every payout is locked in the vault at the moment of purchase. Claims can never exceed reserves.
How it works

One premium in. One of two outcomes out.

✈

Buy cover

A traveler pays a small USDC premium for a specific flight and date — e.g. 50 USDC for 500 USDC of cover.

▣

Collateral locks

The full payout is instantly reserved from the risk vault. The policy is solvent by construction.

◎

Oracle attests

Flight data executors record scheduled and actual arrival on-chain. The threshold does the judging.

◈

Auto settlement

A keeper classifies and settles every flight. No human in the payment loop.

Delayed or cancelled

The traveler claims the fixed payout — immediately, in USDC.

On time

The premium becomes yield for the underwriters who backed the risk.

Two-sided market

Travelers buy certainty.
Underwriters earn the premium.

Travelers

Transparent fixed-payout protection, purchased in one transaction and claimable in one transaction. Terms live on-chain — the product cannot quietly change after purchase.

Underwriters

Deposit USDC into an ERC-4626-style risk vault and hold yield-bearing shares. Premiums from on-time flights accrue to the share price; a FIFO withdrawal queue guarantees orderly, settlement-safe exits.

The protocol itself takes no custody shortcuts: capital sits in the vault, payouts come from locked collateral, and the books must balance on every settlement.

Governance automation

Four watchers. One ledger.
One way back.

Cancellation

A flight has stopped operating — confirmed by sweeping the days around it, not guessed from one lookup.

Exposure

One route or airport is carrying half the vault's capacity. Concentration is capped before it becomes a loss.

Weather

An extreme forecast at either airport. Milder weather raises the premium instead of stopping the sale.

Pricing

The honest price has risen above the cap. We stop selling rather than sell at a known loss.

  • No watcher touches the chain directly. Each one writes its evidence to a single ledger, and only then does one audited path disable the route — so the record survives even if the write fails.
  • Everything that pauses has a way back. An hourly engine re-checks every open hold against its own condition and re-opens the route when the last one clears.
  • A human is always above it — a freeze switch that needs no redeploy, pins the automation cannot override, and on-chain limits only the owner can move.

CALIBRATION ERROR

Predicted rate versus actual rate across the network.

  • It predicts the policy's own event — arrival three hours late, cancelled, or diverted. Not a proxy, the thing we actually pay on.
  • Calibration is the whole point. The premium multiplies this probability, so a score that ranks well but is numerically wrong is a mispriced book, not a minor error.
  • The model never sees insurance terms — it returns a probability, and the protocol turns that into a price with a fixed margin. Above a hard ceiling we decline the route rather than sell it.
Capital efficiency

Every policy backed — without
stranding the capital that backs it.

Bounded in both directions

A purchase is refused unless reserves already cover it — and withdrawals are capped by that same reserve. Guarding only the buy side would let capital walk out through the gap between purchases.

Exits that never jam

First in, first out, with stable positions. When the oldest request is larger than the capital free to pay it, it takes what is available and keeps its place — instead of blocking everyone behind it.

  • Queue work stops while any outcome is unsettled, so the share price never moves on half-finished books.
  • New deposits are processed before exits each cycle — arriving capital can fund a departing underwriter the same pass.
  • Uncollected payouts are not lost. Balances are kept alive on every write, backed by scheduled upkeep, with an owner recovery path behind that.
Decentralization

Sufficiently decentralized now.
Fully decentralized on evidence.

Already open

Storage upkeep, pruning and expiry sweeps are permissionless today. Every keeper bot is public code anyone can run.

One contract change away

Removing the keeper gate and paying bounties on work already reported by the contracts. The loop runs against today's code minus one check.

Never decentralized

Who we are willing to insure, and at what price. That is an underwriting decision, not a consensus problem.

  • Publishing the keepers costs us nothing — they carry no information the contracts don't re-verify. That is precisely why they are already public.
  • The oracle hardens rather than multiplies — a trusted execution environment takes over the same address, instead of adding voters to a quorum.
  • Gated on measurement, not a date. The beta tells us settlement latency and cost per settlement; those numbers size the bounty. Failure stays fail-closed at every step.
